# Producing a research brief

The brief is read by somebody who will rely on it. Currency and the
counter-argument are the two things that make it safe.

## Structure

The question, as asked and as you have understood it · short answer · the
authorities, primary before secondary, each with full citation · the reasoning ·
**the counter-argument** and how strong it is · what remains uncertain and what
would resolve it.

## Currency is not optional

For every authority: whether it is still good law, whether it has been appealed,
distinguished or overruled, and the date checked. An uncited-as-of date makes
the brief unusable next month.

## The counter-argument section

Never omit it. A brief that only supports the client's position is the one that
loses in court, and the fee earner needs to know the other side's best point
before opposing counsel makes it.

## Jurisdiction

State it explicitly, including where a point differs between the jurisdictions
of the same state.

## Never

Cite a case you have not read past the headnote, present secondary commentary as
authority, or invent a citation. A fabricated citation ends careers.

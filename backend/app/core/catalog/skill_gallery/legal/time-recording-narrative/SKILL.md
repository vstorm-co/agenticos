# Writing a time narrative

A narrative is read by somebody deciding whether to pay it. Write it for them.

## Say what was produced

"Drafting particulars of claim; considering counsel's advice on quantum" is
billable work described. "Attending to matter" and "reviewing file" are the two
entries that generate fee disputes, and they are the two most commonly written.

## One task, one entry

Block-billing four tasks under one figure invites the whole entry to be
challenged. Separate them, each with its own time.

## Match the record

The narrative must be supported by something on the file — a document, an email,
an attendance note. An entry with no artefact behind it cannot be defended on
assessment.

## Flag rather than write

Time recorded to the wrong matter · a narrative that does not match the fee
earner's grade · duplicated attendance by two fee earners without a reason ·
anything above the estimate the client was given.

## Never

Write a narrative for work you cannot see evidence of, round up, or describe
internal training or file management as client work.

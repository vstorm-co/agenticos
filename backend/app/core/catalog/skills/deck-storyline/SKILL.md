# The story before the slides

Most weak decks are weak before the first slide: nobody decided what they should
make the audience do. Settle these four things, in this order, and write them
down in your answer before you build anything.

## 1. The audience and the decision

Who is in the room, what do they already know, and what should they do or decide
when it ends? "Inform" is not a decision. If the person cannot say, ask one
question: what should be different tomorrow because of this meeting?

## 2. The answer in one sentence

The sentence the audience should leave with, with the numbers in it:
`Two suppliers carry most of the risk, and one is late on half its orders.` If it
needs two sentences, the deck has two messages - make two decks, or choose.

## 3. The storyline: claims, not topics

List the slides as sentences that each prove part of the answer. Read down the list
of titles alone: it should read as the argument. If it reads as a table of contents
(`Background`, `Methodology`, `Results`), rewrite each as the claim it makes.

A reliable order: the answer first (the audience should not wait for it), why it
matters to them, the evidence in two to five slides, the options if there are any,
the recommendation, the ask. Put the ask in words, with a date and a name.

Five to twelve slides. Cut a slide that does not support the answer, however
interesting; an appendix belongs in a page, linked.

## 4. What goes on the slide, and what in the notes

On the slide: the claim, and the one thing that proves it - a number, a chart, a
comparison. In the notes: how to say it, the source and its date, the question
somebody will ask and the answer to it. Notes are not the slide's text again.

## Using what the person gave you

From a document or a brief, take the answer and the evidence from it rather than
its structure: a report goes from background to results, a deck goes from the
answer to the proof. Quote numbers exactly and say where each came from. Where
a figure is missing or a claim cannot be checked, say so in your answer and leave
the slide out or mark it, never fill it with a plausible number.

## Never

Open with an agenda slide, thank the audience as the last slide instead of the
ask, put the answer on slide eight, or let a slide's title be a single noun.

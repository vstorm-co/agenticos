A short lesson in asking better questions

From question
to evidence.

A useful model starts with something we can observe, not a conclusion we hope to defend.

Five slides · Use Previous/Next or the arrow keys · Example material

01 · Make the question concrete

What would we actually notice?

Does a short break help us solve the next task?

Define the task, the length of the break and what “help” means before collecting results.

Teaching example: not a claim that a particular break improves performance.

02 · Make a fair comparison

Change one condition.
Keep the rest comparable.

Question
A clear outcome

Comparison
Break / no break

Observation
Record the same measure

Task difficulty, prior practice and interruptions can change the result too.

This simplified lesson introduces comparison; it does not replace a study design.

03 · Keep the uncertainty visible

A result is a clue.
It is not the whole story.

  • Show the sample and conditions.
  • Check for another explanation.
  • Separate an observation from an interpretation.
No fabricated measurements are needed to explain the method.

04 · Try it yourself

Choose a question you could test this week.

Write the outcome you would measure, one fair comparison and one thing that could mislead you.

Speaker note

Invite one concrete example before discussing why the comparison is imperfect. Keep the exercise short enough to complete during the presentation.

Next step: adapt the example and include real, attributable sources for factual claims.

# AgenticOS vs Viktor

Viktor is worth evaluating when you want a teammate service in Slack or Teams. AgenticOS is worth evaluating when your team wants to configure agents in a browser and operate the platform on its own infrastructure.

Maintained by the AgenticOS team. Sources checked 25 September 2026. AgenticOS baseline: v0.0.503. Viktor scope: its public product and pricing pages, not a tested account or negotiated enterprise plan.

## The operating choice

| Area | Viktor | AgenticOS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | Vendor describes an AI teammate in Slack and Teams | Build instructions, choose tools and bind document collections |
| Deployment | Product page describes cloud execution | Operator-managed deployment |
| Ongoing work | Verify service support, access and plan terms | Assign upgrades, backups, provider configuration and incident handling |
| Cost | Check the current plan and usage limits | Account for models, infrastructure, integration and operating time |

[Viktor's product description](https://viktor.com/) and [pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing) are the sources for its offering. AgenticOS configuration and responsibilities are described in [your first agent](../first-agent.md) and [operation](../rollout.md).

## Try one handbook question

Use the [shared document fixture](../howto/first-document-agent.md). Compare source access, the actual answer, a missing-policy question and a source update. Check which identity can retrieve the source and how access is removed.

Choose the service route if its tested task fit and operating terms meet your needs. Choose an owned deployment if control over configuration and infrastructure is worth the maintenance responsibility. Neither route establishes better answer quality by itself.

No shared workload was run here. Setup time, answer quality, approval coverage and effective cost remain unmeasured. Use the [comparison method](comparison.md) to record them.
